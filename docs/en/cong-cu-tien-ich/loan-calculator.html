How to use the Loan Calculator

  1. Choose the interest method: declining balance, equal monthly payments or flat rate.
  2. Enter the loan amount, term and rate. If there's a promotion, tick the promo rate box and enter the number of months.
  3. See the first payment, total interest and monthly schedule, with a comparison of the three methods below.

Three common ways to calculate loan interest

  • Declining balance (equal principal): each month you pay principal = Loan ÷ Months, plus interest = Remaining balance × Rate ÷ 12. The first payment is the largest, then it decreases. This is the most common method for bank home and car loans in Vietnam.
  • Equal monthly payments (annuity): a fixed monthly payment = Loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where r is the monthly rate and n the number of months. Easy to budget for, but total interest is higher than equal principal.
  • Flat rate: interest is always charged on the original loan amount, even after you've repaid some principal. Common for consumer loans and installment purchases. The effective rate is nearly double the advertised figure — the tool converts it so you can compare.

Frequently asked questions

What's the monthly payment on a 1 billion VND loan over 20 years?
At 9%/year on a declining balance, the first payment is about 11,666,667 VND (principal 4,166,667 + interest 7,500,000), then it decreases. With equal payments, it's about 8,997,000 VND a month.
What happens when the promotional rate ends?
After the promo period, the bank switches to a floating rate, usually the base rate plus the margin in your contract. Enter the expected post-promo rate in the Interest rate box to see your later payments.
Declining balance or equal payments?
Equal principal has lower total interest but higher payments early on. Equal payments are lighter at first but cost more interest overall. The comparison table shows the exact difference for your loan.
What is a 1%/month flat rate really worth?
A 12%/year flat rate on a 12-month loan equals about 21.5%/year on a declining-balance basis, because interest is always charged on the full original amount.
