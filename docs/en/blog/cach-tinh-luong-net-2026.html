How to calculate net salary in 2026 under the new income tax rules

From 2026, Vietnam's personal income tax has just 5 brackets and the personal deduction rises to 15.5 million VND/month. Most salaried employees will take home more than in 2025 — some nearly 1 million VND more per month.

This article explains how to calculate net (take-home) salary from gross salary (the amount in your contract), with concrete examples so you can check your payslip or negotiate salary at interviews.

Don't want to do the math? Enter your salary, number of dependants and region to instantly see net pay, tax per bracket and employer cost. Open the Gross ↔ Net salary calculator →

Net salary formula

Net salary = Gross salary − Mandatory insurance − Personal income tax

  1. Employee insurance: 10.5% of the insured salary — social insurance 8%, health insurance 1.5%, unemployment insurance 1%.
  2. Taxable income = Gross − Insurance − 15.5 million (personal) − 6.2 million × number of dependants.
  3. Personal income tax is progressive, using the 5-bracket table below. If taxable income is ≤ 0, no tax is due.

5-bracket income tax table from 2026

  • Bracket 1: up to 10 million/month — 5%
  • Bracket 2: over 10 to 30 million — 10%
  • Bracket 3: over 30 to 60 million — 20%
  • Bracket 4: over 60 to 100 million — 30%
  • Bracket 5: over 100 million — 35%

Progressive means each bracket's rate only applies to the income within that bracket. For example, with 15 million of taxable income: the first 10 million is taxed at 5% (500,000 VND) and the remaining 5 million at 10% (500,000 VND), for a total of 1,000,000 VND.

Insurance contribution cap

High earners don't pay 10.5% on their entire salary:

  • Social and health insurance are capped at 20 times the reference level: 50.6 million from 1 July 2026 (based on 2,530,000 VND), and 46.8 million in the first half of the year.
  • Unemployment insurance is capped at 20 times the regional minimum wage: 106.2 million in Region I (5,310,000 VND × 20).

Many companies only pay insurance on part of the salary (for example, the regional minimum wage). Insurance is then lower but tax is slightly higher — the salary calculator has an Other amount field for exactly this case.

Net salary examples for 2026

The examples below are for Region I, with insurance paid on the full salary, effective from 1 July 2026.

Gross salary 20 million, no dependants

  • Insurance: 20,000,000 × 10.5% = 2,100,000 VND
  • Taxable income: 20,000,000 − 2,100,000 − 15,500,000 = 2,400,000 VND
  • Tax: 2,400,000 × 5% = 120,000 VND
  • Take-home pay: 17,780,000 VND

Gross salary 30 million, 1 dependant

  • Insurance: 3,150,000 VND
  • Taxable income: 30,000,000 − 3,150,000 − 15,500,000 − 6,200,000 = 5,150,000 VND
  • Tax: 5,150,000 × 5% = 257,500 VND
  • Take-home pay: 26,592,500 VND (with no dependants: 26,215,000 VND)

Gross salary 50 million, no dependants

  • Insurance: 50,000,000 × 10.5% = 5,250,000 VND (below the 50.6 million cap)
  • Taxable income: 50,000,000 − 5,250,000 − 15,500,000 = 29,250,000 VND
  • Tax: 10 million × 5% + 19.25 million × 10% = 2,425,000 VND
  • Take-home pay: 42,325,000 VND

Compared with 2025: how much more do you get?

In 2025 the deductions were 11 million (personal) and 4.4 million (per dependant), with a 7-bracket table starting at 5 million. For the same salary with no dependants:

  • Gross 20 million: tax 440,000 → 120,000 VND. Take-home pay rises by 320,000 VND/month.
  • Gross 30 million: tax 1,627,500 → 635,000 VND. Take-home pay rises by 992,500 VND/month.

At what salary do you start paying tax?

With no dependants and insurance paid on the full salary, tax only applies once gross pay exceeds about 17.3 million/month. With 1 dependant: above about 24.2 million. Each additional dependant raises the threshold by nearly 7 million.

Working backwards: what gross salary should you ask for?

When an employer asks for your expected gross salary but you only know the take-home amount you want, work backwards. For example, to take home 25 million (no dependants) you need a gross salary of about 28.5 million. The salary calculator has a Net → Gross tab that gives the exact figure down to the last dong.

How much does the employer pay?

On top of the gross salary, the company pays an extra 21.5% in insurance (social 17.5%, health 3%, unemployment 1%), not counting the 2% trade union fee. For a 20 million gross salary, the total employer cost is about 24.3 million/month.

What happens to the social insurance you've paid?

The 8% social insurance deducted each month adds to your contribution record. If you leave your job and qualify for a lump-sum withdrawal, the amount depends on your average salary and years of contributions — use the lump-sum social insurance calculator to estimate it. If you're planning a home or car loan, use your net salary as the baseline, try monthly payments with the loan calculator, and see how much interest your savings could earn with the savings calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Who counts as a dependant?
Usually children under 18 or still in school, a spouse, or parents past working age — provided they have no income or income below the legal threshold. Dependants must be registered with your company or the tax office to qualify for the deduction.

Are lunch and phone allowances taxed?
Some allowances are tax-exempt up to set limits. The calculator treats the whole gross amount you enter as taxable — if you have tax-exempt allowances, subtract them before entering.

Can the result replace my payslip?
No — it's an estimate based on general rules, for reference and salary negotiation. The official figures are your company's payroll and the year-end tax finalization.
