How to Write Amounts in Words in Vietnamese — Rules and Examples

Writing the amount in words on an invoice, contract or payment order looks simple, but it is easy to get words like “lẻ”, “mốt”, “lăm” and “không trăm” wrong. This guide sums up all the rules, with an example table and common mistakes.

Writing the amount in words helps prevent tampering with documents: a figure can have an extra 0 added, while a line of words is much harder to alter. That is why invoices, contracts, receipts, payment vouchers and deposit slips usually require both the figure and the words.

Rule 1: split the number into groups of three digits

Split the number from right to left into groups of three digits, just like where the dots go: 15.750.000 has three groups: 15, 750 and 000.

Each group is read as a number from 0 to 999, followed by the name of its place. From lowest to highest, the place names are: đơn vị (units), nghìn (thousand), triệu (million), tỷ (billion) and nghìn tỷ (thousand billion).

Example: 15.750.000 is read “mười lăm triệu bảy trăm năm mươi nghìn”. The last group is 000, so nothing more is read.

Rule 2: “mười” and “mươi”

  • Mười is used for numbers from 10 to 19: mười, mười một, mười hai, mười chín.
  • Mươi is used for tens from 20 upward: hai mươi, ba mươi, chín mươi.
  • Round tens are read in full: 50 is “năm mươi”, 70 is “bảy mươi”.

Rule 3: “mốt”, “tư” and “lăm” in the units place

The digit in the units place changes how it is read depending on the tens digit before it. This is where most handwritten mistakes happen.

  • Mốt: a 1 after “mươi” is read “mốt” (21 is hai mươi mốt, 91 is chín mươi mốt). After “mười” it is still read “một” (11 is mười một).
  • Tư: a 4 after “mươi” is usually read “tư” (24 is hai mươi tư). After “mười” it is read “bốn” (14 is mười bốn).
  • Lăm: a 5 after “mười” or “mươi” is read “lăm” (15 is mười lăm, 35 is ba mươi lăm).
  • When the tens digit is 0, these digits keep their original reading: 105 is một trăm lẻ năm, 104 is một trăm lẻ bốn.

Rule 4: “lẻ” (or “linh”) when the tens digit is 0

When the hundreds digit is non-zero, the tens digit is 0 and the units digit is not 0, add the word “lẻ” in between. For example, 101 is “một trăm lẻ một” and 308 is “ba trăm lẻ tám”.

In northern Vietnam people often say “linh” instead of “lẻ” (một trăm linh một). Both are correct, but use only one consistently on a single document.

Rule 5: “không trăm” in later groups

If a three-digit group that follows another group has a value below 100, you must say “không trăm” (zero hundred) explicitly. If you leave it out, readers will misunderstand the value of the number.

  • 1.050.000 is “một triệu không trăm năm mươi nghìn”, not “một triệu năm mươi nghìn”.
  • 2.005.000 is “hai triệu không trăm lẻ năm nghìn”.
  • A group of 000 is skipped entirely: 1.000.500 is “một triệu năm trăm”.

How to write the amount in words on invoices and contracts

  • Capitalize the first letter of the line; write the rest in lowercase.
  • Add the currency unit at the end, for example “đồng”. Many businesses also add “chẵn” when the amount has no odd remainder: “Một triệu hai trăm năm mươi nghìn đồng chẵn”.
  • Put it in parentheses after the figure in a contract: 1.250.000 đồng (Bằng chữ: Một triệu hai trăm năm mươi nghìn đồng chẵn).
  • Choose “nghìn” or “ngàn” and use it consistently throughout the document. Invoices usually use “nghìn”.
  • Check the figure against the words before signing. If they don't match, the document may be rejected or lead to a dispute.

In employment contracts, the salary is usually written both in figures and in words. If you're not sure of the take-home pay, work it out first with the Gross ↔ Net salary calculator, then convert it to words.

Decimals and foreign currencies

Vietnamese dong has no decimal part, so VND amounts only need the whole number read. For ordinary decimals, the part after the comma is read with “phẩy” (point), followed by each digit: 1,25 is “một phẩy hai năm”.

Each foreign currency has its own conventions; for example, 1.250,50 USD is usually written “Một nghìn hai trăm năm mươi đô la Mỹ và năm mươi xu”. The OneTool converter focuses on VND amounts, so for foreign currencies you should write the unit correctly yourself.

Common mistakes

  • Writing “mười năm” instead of “mười lăm”, or “hai mươi một” instead of “hai mươi mốt”.
  • Forgetting “không trăm”, so 1.050.000 is misread as 1.000.050.
  • Forgetting “lẻ” and writing “một trăm năm” for 105 (easily mistaken for 150).
  • Mixing “nghìn” and “ngàn”, or “lẻ” and “linh”, in the same document.
  • Copying a number from a spreadsheet with comma separators (1,250,000) and misreading it as a decimal.

Frequently asked questions

Should I write “lẻ” or “linh”?
Both are correct. “Lẻ” is common on financial documents, while “linh” is more common in northern Vietnam. What matters is using one consistently within a document.

Is 24 read “hai mươi tư” or “hai mươi bốn”?
Both are accepted. “Hai mươi tư” is more common in writing, but 14 is always read “mười bốn”.

Is writing “chẵn” required?
No. The word “chẵn” only emphasizes that the amount has no odd remainder; many accountants add it out of habit to prevent tampering.

How is 1.000.000.000.000 read?
It is read “một nghìn tỷ” (one thousand billion). Vietnamese usually has no separate word for a trillion.
